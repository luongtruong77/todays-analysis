If the Falcons dominate

ATL 36–19 CIN 16% chance · avg margin 16.6

Atlanta win big only if the game is played their way: Bijan Robinson (#7) and Khalil Herbert (#25) run at a Cincinnati defence that is only middling against the run (−0.011 EPA per carry), Atlanta control the clock and keep Burrow on the sideline. What has to happen

  • Bijan over 100 rushing yards and Atlanta winning time of possession.
  • Atlanta plus-two in turnovers, Burrow repeating his three-interception game.
  • London over 90 yards against single coverage off play-action.
  • Atlanta score TDs, not field goals, in the red zone against a Cincinnati defence that allows just 40%.

If the Bengals dominate

CIN 37–18 ATL 34% chance · avg margin 18.4

Cincinnati's offence is the best unit on the field (+0.223 EPA per dropback) against a pass defence that is merely average (−0.002), and the Bengals barely run the ball (65% pass rate, −0.088 per carry). If Joe Burrow (#9) keeps up his last three weeks (4, 4 and 5 TD passes), Ja'Marr Chase (#1) and Tee Higgins (#5) win outside, and Cincinnati turn red-zone trips into TDs, Atlanta have to chase. That is the bad version for Atlanta: Michael Penix Jr. (#9) leads a pass offence at −0.161 EPA per dropback, so falling behind takes away Bijan Robinson (#7), the one thing that works, and invites turnovers.

What has to happen

  • Burrow at 3+ TD passes, with Chase or Higgins over 100 yards.
  • Cincinnati keep converting in the red zone (62%) while holding Atlanta to field goals; the Bengals allow TDs on only 40% of trips.
  • Cincinnati lead by two scores at halftime, so Atlanta abandon the run and Penix throws 40+ times.
  • A Penix interception or a strip sack sets up a short field.

Method & caveats

  • Inactives here are projected from Friday's reports. Recheck around 5 AM PT, especially Herbert (#25): if he is out, Bijan's carries go up in both scripts.
  • Scenario probabilities come from the market (Pinnacle no-vig ML and spread, margin σ = 13.5), not the app's models, which trail the closing line.
  • Player projections start from each prop's market median and apply rule-of-thumb game-script multipliers (the leader runs more, the trailer throws more). They are not fitted to data, so read them as directional.
  • Scenario legs are conditional bets: they only make sense if you think that script is likelier than the market says. Unconditionally they are no better than the market price; the measured edge (Falcons +3.5) is on the main analysis.
  • Two pairs share numbers on opposite teams: Burrow and Penix both wear #9; Higgins and London both wear #5.
  • Neutral site: the efficiency numbers come from games with normal home and away travel, and no roof decision or forecast was found.
